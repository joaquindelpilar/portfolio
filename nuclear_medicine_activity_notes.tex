\section{Activity Formula}

The activity of a radioactive sample is
\[
\boxed{A=-\frac{dN}{dt}}
\]
or equivalently,
\[
\boxed{A=\lambda N}
\]
where
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item $A$ = activity,
    \item $N$ = number of radioactive nuclei,
    \item $\lambda$ = decay constant.
\end{itemize}

\section{Example: Technetium-99m}

Given a Technetium-99m sample with a half-life of
\[
T_{1/2}=6\ \text{h}
\]
and
\[
N=1.0\times10^{15}\ \text{radioactive nuclei}.
\]

\subsection*{Step 1: Convert hours to seconds}
\[
6\ \text{h}\left(\frac{3600\ \text{s}}{1\ \text{h}}\right)=21600\ \text{s}
\]

\subsection*{Step 2: Calculate the decay constant}
Using
\[
\lambda=\frac{\ln 2}{T_{1/2}},
\]
we get
\[
\lambda=\frac{\ln 2}{21600\ \text{s}}
=3.21\times10^{-5}\ \text{s}^{-1}.
\]

\subsection*{Step 3: Calculate the activity}
\[
A=\lambda N
\]
\[
A=(3.21\times10^{-5}\ \text{s}^{-1})(1.0\times10^{15})
\]
\[
\boxed{A=3.21\times10^{10}\ \text{Bq}}
\]

\section{Curie and Radioactivity Notes}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item Curie discovered radium and polonium.
    \item The term ``radioactivity'' was coined by Marie Curie.
    \item One curie (Ci) is
    \[
    \boxed{1\ \text{Ci}=3.70\times10^{10}\ \text{disintegrations per second (dps)}}.
    \]
    \item The handwritten source also notes that this corresponds to the activity associated with approximately 1 g of radium-226.
\end{itemize}
